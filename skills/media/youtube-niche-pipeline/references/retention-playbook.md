# Retention Playbook

How the script markers map to what keeps a long-form viewer watching. Use it
when writing or revising a script that `script_tools.py check` flagged.

## Pacing for a 2,500-word script

At about 150 words per minute, 2,500 words is roughly 16-17 minutes.

| Time | Words | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | 0-40 | `[HOOK]`: most surprising moment plus a promised payoff |
| 0:15-1:30 | 40-220 | Context: why this matters now, and a first `[SUB-HOOK]` |
| 1:30-12:00 | 220-1,800 | 3-4 escalating beats, each closing on a `[SUB-HOOK]` |
| ~12:00 | ~1,800 | Biggest reveal, an `[EMOTIONAL PEAK]` |
| 12:00-15:00 | 1,800-2,250 | Counterpoint: what would make this wrong |
| 15:00-end | 2,250-2,500 | Payoff and a closing tease for the next video |

Put a `[PATTERN INTERRUPT]` every 350-450 words. Put the `[CTA]` after the
first real payoff, usually around 3-5 minutes in.

## Hooks (first 15 seconds)

- **Cold open mid-action:** "Thirty-one seconds into the trailer, there's a
  license plate nobody talked about."
- **Stakes:** "If this leak is right, GTA 6 Online launches without heists."
- **Contradiction:** "Rockstar said the map is smaller. The files say otherwise."
- **Countdown urgency:** "In four days, millions of players will miss this."

Never open with a greeting, a channel intro, or "in this video".

## Sub-hooks (open a new loop before closing the old one)

- "But that's not even the strangest part."
- "Hold on to that detail. It matters in about two minutes."
- "And that's where the theory falls apart... or so everyone thought."

## Pattern interrupts (break the rhythm)

- A direct question: "Be honest. Did you catch that the first time?"
- A quick-fire list: "Three things. The clock. The plate. The billboard."
- A tone flip: drop from dramatic to deadpan for one line.
- A rewind: "Wait. Go back to that frame."
- A scene change: a `[VISUAL:]` hard cut to a new location or a map overlay.

## Emotional peaks

Use awe (scale of the map, a beautiful shot), tension (a deadline, a cut
feature), nostalgia (a callback to Vice City 2002 or GTA V), or vindication
("the community called this a year ago"). Slow the sentences down going in.
Let one short line land on its own.

## Titles and thumbnails

- Keep titles to 60 characters or fewer, with the keyword early and one emotion per title.
- Formulas: "GTA 6's [X] Is Hiding Something", "Nobody Noticed This in GTA 6",
  "Rockstar Just Changed [X]... Here's Why", "The GTA 6 [X] Theory Is Real?"
- Thumbnail text: 4 words or fewer, and never a repeat of the title. Title
  and thumbnail should work as a pair: one sets up the question, the other
  raises the stakes.

## Platform rules to respect

Check these against YouTube's current help pages. Policies change.

- **Monetization:** YouTube's Partner Program excludes "inauthentic"
  (mass-produced or repetitive) content. Each video needs original analysis
  and a point of view. Don't paste Reddit posts into a voice.
- **Altered or synthetic content:** disclose in YouTube Studio when the video
  shows realistic AI-generated people, events, or places that could be mistaken
  for real. Plain AI narration over gameplay usually does not need it, but check.
- **Copyright:** use official trailers, screenshots, and your own gameplay
  capture. Rockstar has taken down leaked footage before. Describe leaks, and
  never show leaked video or assets. Commentary and transformation help
  fair-use claims but do not guarantee them. This is not legal advice.
- **Spoilers:** after launch, put "SPOILERS" in the title or thumbnail of any
  story or ending video.
